# Available Data

> This document summarizes the data used for the investigation.

We do not discuss specifics of how the data pipeline works, since
that has already been discussed in
[the README](../../README.md) of this repository. Rather, our
goal here is to explain roughly which data is available and why.

Due to other investigations related to our collaboration with
Giga, we already had data for Moldova for 2026-05. We chose
to extend the data collection to Malawi, since communications
with the Giga team suggested this was an interesting case with
lots of unexplained bits, due to the use of LEO satellites.

We ran the original analysis in this repository based only on
those two countries and 2026-05. We drew initial conclusions
that were shared with the Giga team in 2026-06. Afterwards,
in 2026-09, we extended the data collection to the date range
between 2026-01 and 2026-07 (both included).

This period of time includes school vacations. In Moldova,
the 2025-2026 school year ended on 2026-05-29, so June and
July fall within the summer vacation. In Malawi, the 2025/26
academic year ran until late July 2026, so schools were in
session for most of the window, apart from term breaks.

Additionally, the number of distinct schools appearing in the
data each month is roughly stable in Malawi, while in Moldova
it peaks in February-March and declines during the summer. The
volume of Malawi tests increased markedly from 2026-05 onwards
without a corresponding increase in the number of active
schools.

These facts imply that the population of active schools and
the volume of tests change across the observation window.
Apparent trends over time may therefore reflect changes in
who is testing rather than changes in network performance,
and the analysis in this repository should be read with this
caveat in mind.

A second limitation concerns how we join the data sources.
The Superset query selects tests by the school's country
(`country_code`), while the M-Lab query selects tests by
M-Lab's geolocation of the client IP address
(`client.Geo.CountryCode`), in addition to requiring
`client_name` to be `giga-meter`. Of the 58,101 Superset
download tests in the 2026-01 to 2026-07 window, 4,233 are
missing from the M-Lab export, and 4,213 of those are
Malawi tests (about 19% of the 22,314 Malawi tests). The
missing tests are spread across the whole window, so they are
not a date-boundary artifact, though the missing share of
Malawi tests declines over time (about 37% in 2026-01 and
about 10% in 2026-07).

The cause is the `client_name` filter: Giga meter only
declares `client_name` as `giga_meter` since vrsion 2.0.2:
tests run from older clients do not match the query. The
declining share of tests reflects schools upgrading
their Giga Meter installation. The remaining 33 Malawi
and 22 Moldova missing tests have not been
investigated. Our working hypothesis is a geolocation mismatch.

This is a data quality problem, since the missing tests are
not a random sample. The 2.0.1 tests come from 83 of the 166
Malawi schools in the window. According to the TCPInfo data
stored by `giga-meter`, their median `MinRTT` is about 87 ms,
compared to about 71 ms for the joined Malawi tests, and their
median client goodput is about half (7.7 versus 14.8 Mbit/s).
Their client reports a duration above 15 s only slightly more
often (4.3% versus 3.9%).

The tests we analyze therefore under-represent high-latency,
low-throughput Malawi paths, especially in earlier months and
with a reduced impact in later months. The missing tests
could be recovered by querying BigQuery by UUID, since the
Superset export has the UUIDs. (These numbers come from
ad-hoc queries on the local data and are not yet
printed by any script.)
