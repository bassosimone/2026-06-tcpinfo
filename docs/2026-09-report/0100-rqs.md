# Research Questions

> This document summarizes the original research questions that
> we aim to answer, or get a better understanding of, with the
> investigation carried out in this repository.

The original research question is simple. Why does the total
runtime of the `giga-meter` client
(`unicef/project-connect-daily-check-app>=v2.0.2` bundling
`m-lab/ndt7-js==0.0.6`) vary significantly across tests?

Two additional research questions arose when investigating:

1. Rather than speculating purely by looking at the client
implementation, can we explain and characterize this
behavior using the other available data sources, that is,
the kernel-level data collected by `m-lab/ndt-server`
and by the `m-lab/tcp-info` sidecar?

2. What conclusions can we draw from this investigation that
could support improving the `ndt7` specification and its
implementations, or inform the design of the next-generation
NDT protocol?

Across this investigation, we used a specific "tier" convention
to label the available data sources. The general idea is that of
classifying the data sources by how close they are to the
underlying ground truth. Hence:

1. Tier 1 (T1) is the data collected by `m-lab/tcp-info`, since
this data is as close as possible to the kernel and does not
suffer from the implementation issues affecting
`m-lab/ndt-server` (notably, it keeps sampling while
`ndt-server` is blocked on writing; see
[the ndt7 document](0000-ndt7.md))

2. Tier 2 (T2) is the data collected by `m-lab/ndt-server`,
which has the aforementioned implementation issues and yet
is the data source from which `giga-meter` collects
its unique TCPInfo/BBRInfo sample

3. Tier 3 (T3) is the data collected by `giga-meter` and
submitted to Giga's own servers (we access this data through
Giga's Superset). This data includes the client-side AppInfo
as well as the latest TCPInfo/BBRInfo metrics that the server
sent to the client

To reason about this data, one needs to take into account that
there is an inherent delay between the moment one party collects
a piece of data and the moment the other party observes it.
Because of the head-of-line blocking discussed in
[the ndt7 document](0000-ndt7.md), a specific piece of data may
be queued for quite some time. In addition, even if there were
no such head-of-line blocking, there would still be queueing in
the network and latency.
